Emergency signs: Fever and weakness in dogs and cats can signal illness and should be evaluated quickly.
If your pet has a fever, appears weak, is not eating, has trouble breathing, or collapses, contact a veterinarian or emergency clinic immediately. Keep the animal calm and avoid human medication unless directed by a vet.

Common wellness checks: Routine veterinary exams, vaccines, and preventive care help catch illness early.
Healthy pets should have normal hydration, steady appetite, and alert behavior. Any sudden change in eating, drinking, litter box habits, or energy can be a warning sign.

Diet and hydration: Pets need consistent, appropriate nutrition and access to clean water.
Do not switch foods abruptly. Gradual transitions over several days reduce stomach upset. Dehydration can worsen quickly, especially in older pets and puppies.

Medication and treatment: Only use medicines approved for the species and dosage prescribed by a professional.
Never give human painkillers, antihistamines, or supplements without veterinary instruction; many are toxic to cats and dogs.

Behavior and stress: Restless or hidden behavior can indicate discomfort or anxiety.
Monitor sleep patterns, appetite, elimination, and playfulness. Stress can be worsened by loud environments, routine changes, or untreated pain.

Post-visit care: Follow-up care matters after illness, surgery, or treatment changes.
Provide a quiet space, maintain medications as directed, and contact your veterinarian if the pet’s symptoms worsen.
